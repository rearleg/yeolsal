## 3. Successes (What Went Well)

### S1 — Codegen pipeline made FE↔BE design drift structurally impossible

Story 1.5 shipped `FE/src/theme/tokens.json` + `BE/.../tokens.schema.json` + Gradle `validateTokens` / `generateTokens` tasks + Checkstyle hex-literal guard. Stories 1.6 (`<WelcomeWindow>`) and 1.7 (`<RitualMoment>`) consumed the foundation immediately with zero drift. The packed-type `semantic.survival.{ACTIVE|YELLOW|RED|SPECTATOR}` enforcement means a future story **cannot** ship a color-without-label violation — `tools/brand-voice-lint.ts` Rule 1 rejects it at PR time.

**Evidence:** Story 1.6 wrote `<SurvivalChip state="YELLOW">` end-to-end; Story 1.7 read `theme.color.key.glow` and `theme.color.key.muted` from `useTheme()` and never touched a hex literal.

### S2 — Survival service evolved additively across three stories without regressions

`SurvivalStateService` shipped in 1.1 (`initializeOnJoin`, `inGraceWindow`), gained `evaluateRoom` in 1.2 (constructor widened to 11 args), and `roster` in 1.3 (read-only method on the same class with no constructor change). The 195 → 233 → 265 test count progression has zero failures on the inherited methods at each step.

**Evidence:** Story 1.3 Completion Notes — *"`SurvivalStateService` constructor and existing methods (`initializeOnJoin`, `inGraceWindow`, `evaluateRoom`) preserved verbatim per BE-2.1 / Story 1.1 + 1.2 contracts. Story 1.1's `SurvivalStateServiceTest` and Story 1.2's `SurvivalStateServiceEvaluateRoomTest` were not touched and still pass."*

### S3 — TDD discipline held throughout the epic with AC-mapped test names

Every story's test method names mirror its AC text (e.g., `roster_nonLeaderNonSelf_sees_masked_red_during_cooldown`, `evaluateRoom_missWithFreezeAlreadyUsed_yellowMemberInGrace_clampsToYellow`, `v11_backfills_room_rule_versions_for_every_legacy_room`). This made review faster and made the AC↔test map auditable.

**Evidence:** Story 1.3 wrote 12 unit tests verbatim from AC12. Story 1.4 wrote 9 ITs matching AC11. Story 1.7 wrote 24 helper tests + 18 component tests across the variant matrix.

### S4 — Native upsert pattern (`INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`) replaced exception-driven idempotency

Story 1.1's Codex review forced the move from `try/catch DataIntegrityViolationException` to `SurvivalStateRepository.insertIfAbsent` (V8/V9 pattern). Stories 1.2 (`streak_freezes`, `notification_log`) and 1.4 (V11 backfill steps 13/14/15) carried the pattern forward consistently.

**Evidence:** Story 1.2 BE-1.2 — `StreakFreezeRepository.insertIfAbsent` mirrors `GroupWarningRepository.insertIfAbsent` shape exactly.

### S5 — `Clock` injection precedent prevented timestamp drift

Every survival service method takes one `Instant now = clock.instant()` snapshot at the top and threads it through state transitions. Story 1.1's Codex review even patched `RoomMember.joined_at = clock.instant()` (added `setJoinedAt` setter) so `grace_ends_at = joined_at + 14d` shares one instant — eliminating a class of "two reads, two timestamps, broken invariant" bugs.

### S6 — Foundation-first Sprint A unblocked downstream stories early

Story 1.5 was split into 4 sub-PRs (Sprints A/B/C/D). Sprint A shipped the BE codegen spine alone so Stories 6.1 / 7.1 could begin consuming `GeneratedTokens` constants without waiting for the FE primitives. This was a deliberate decision driven by the fact-forcing-hook turn-cost across ~30 files.

### S7 — Brand-voice lint caught zero hard violations on new copy across 4 stories

`tools/brand-voice-lint.ts` Rule 2 (AVOID lexicon: `벌금 / 잃었다 / 떨어졌다 / 실패 / 자책 / 부담 / 패배 / 죄책감`) ran on every new Korean string in 1.5, 1.6, 1.7. Reports 0 hard violations on the new surface across all three stories. The 93 warnings are all pre-existing baseline noise (`tokens.ts` legacy literals + 4 form-error copy lines owned by Story 8.2).

## 4. Challenges (What Didn't Go Well)

### C1 — Docker unavailability on the dev environment caused a production hotfix

The single most expensive issue of the epic.

- Story 1.3 IT (`SurvivalStateRosterIT`) was deferred — *"Docker daemon not running in this environment."*
- Story 1.4 IT (`V11MigrationIT`) was deferred — *"Docker is unavailable in this dev environment."*
- The V11 audit (Story 1.4 BE-3) signed off without a green opt-in IT run.
- **2026-05-13 first real production cutover failed at V11 line 68** with `SQLSTATE 42P17 — functions in index expression must be marked IMMUTABLE`. Root cause: `((eliminated_at)::date)` is STABLE, not IMMUTABLE.
- Forward-fixed in-place (V11 had not yet been applied anywhere that mattered), but the audit was retroactively annotated: *"never sign off a V11-class audit without a green opt-in IT run."*

**Why this matters for Epic 2:** Stories 2.1/2.2/2.3 will add chat-write rejection, digest push idempotency, and record visibility — every one of which needs a Testcontainers IT against real Postgres. If Docker stays unavailable, the same class of bug ships again.

### C2 — "Done" was flipped before review findings were resolved on three stories

- Story 1.3 — 4 unresolved `[ ] [Review][Patch]` items at done (per-member lazy user loads vs AC10 4-SQL budget; `@WithMockUser` vs `CurrentUser.require(UserPrincipal)`; build.gradle property forwarding; IT email uniqueness collisions). The last one was fixed retroactively in Story 1.4 BE-2.
- Story 1.6 — 4 unresolved `[ ] [Review][Patch]` items at done (chat publish rollback contract; today tagline flag never consumed/cleared; `roomName` ignored despite AC2; disabled CTA tooltip path).
- Story 1.7 — 4 unresolved `[ ] [Review][Patch]` items at done (Android a11y trap; caption format; T+0.2s announcement timing; `body.lg` token usage).

12 findings across 3 stories were filed by the reviewer but the sprint-status was flipped `review → done` before they were addressed. The done flag is currently misleading.

### C3 — Pre-existing FE baseline failures became a recurring tax

`FriendsTodayPager.tsx` `react-native-pager-view` typecheck miss (inherited from PR #22) and 4 ESLint errors in `rooms/[id]/chat.tsx`, `SurvivalChip.*.test.tsx`, `InviteCodeSheet.test.tsx`, `realtime/client.ts` (inherited from commit `63d7f99`) were declared "out of scope" in Stories 1.2, 1.3, 1.4, 1.5, 1.6, 1.7 — **six times**. Nobody fixed them. Every story owner spent prose justifying the exclusion in their Completion Notes.

### C4 — Sub-mode whitelist desync between epics.md and UX spec caught at W1 design lock

The epics.md AC for Story 1.5 listed 12 sub-mode override keys, but UX §Sub-Mode Catalog D3 Quiet uses 3 keys outside that list AND D5 Plate uses `color.stroke.default` (not in either list). Resolved at implementation time by expanding to the 16-key whitelist documented in Dev Notes, but the cross-reference back to `epics.md` Story 1.5 AC L240 still wasn't patched.

**Pattern:** PRD/UX/epics.md drift caught at implementation, not at planning review.

### C5 — Story 1.5 size required mid-flight split

Story 1.5 was 577 lines of spec, ~30 files of implementation. The fact-forcing-hook fires on every Edit/Write and demands a fresh facts-block + Grep/Glob proof per file, which multiplied turn-cost across the whole story. Mid-flight decision: split into 4 sub-PRs (Sprints A/B/C/D).

This worked, but the *upfront estimate* didn't account for hook turn-cost. Future foundation stories should be sized against the hook surface, not lines of code.

### C6 — Spec deviation in Story 1.6 reveals spec/canon mismatch

Story 1.6 BE-2.1(d) asked for the chat-system-message hook to share a transactional boundary with the membership write — failure rolls back the join. The implementer correctly deviated to `@Transactional(propagation = REQUIRES_NEW)` to match the canonical `ChatService.publishSystem` pattern (mirrors `DailyService.publishGoalSystemMessages`). Outcome: a chat-write failure does NOT roll back the membership insert, which preserves the project-wide "system fan-out is best-effort" invariant.

**The deviation was correct** but it exposes a gap: the spec writer didn't know about the canonical pattern. `project-context.md` does not codify "system fan-out is best-effort" anywhere — every future spec writer can make the same mistake.

### C7 — AC10 batch SQL performance budget was deferred in Story 1.2

Story 1.2 AC10 calls for `< 5 min wall-clock for 50,000 active members` via CTE-based batch SQL. The 2026-05-12 Codex review finding #3 explicitly deferred this: *"current per-member loop fits within the v1 scale budget (1k users × 5 members ≈ 5k member-evals; 4 queries/member; well under 300ms/room ceiling). The AC10 50k-member CTE rewrite is filed as a future-story prerequisite to production cutover at scale."*

The deferral is documented and accepted, but it is **silently load-bearing for production cutover at scale**. No story currently owns the rewrite.

### C8 — Story 1.3 AC10 has the same shape of unfixed perf finding

The privacy-filtered roster service still performs per-member lazy user loads, so AC10's four-SQL budget is not met. The review patch is in `[ ] [Review][Patch]` state, not done. Same risk profile as C7.

## 5. Key Insights

### I1 — Opt-in IT gating works in principle but fails without Docker

`@EnabledIfSystemProperty(named="yeosal.boot-smoke", matches="true")` + `BE/build.gradle:37` `systemProperty` forwarding (Story 1.4) is structurally correct. It silently no-ops on Docker-free environments, which is the intended behavior. The failure mode is *forgetting that it no-ops* — Stories 1.3 and 1.4 both shipped with deferred ITs and the V11 bug slipped through because of it. **Opt-in is not a substitute for CI enforcement.**

### I2 — Codex / adversarial review catches what TDD misses

Every story that went through Codex review came back with substantive findings (3–5 per story). The findings were almost never about test correctness — they were about contract correctness: dedup key scope (1.2 #1), STOMP destination convention (1.2 #2), test infrastructure (1.3 #2/#3/#4), build script behavior (1.3 #3), production data safety (1.4 #4), best-effort invariants (1.6 review #1).

TDD ensures *the implementation does what the test says*. Adversarial review ensures *the test says the right thing*. Both layers are mandatory.

### I3 — The same canonical patterns recur every story

- Native upsert (`INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`) — used in 1.1, 1.2, 1.4.
- `Clock` injection + one `Instant` snapshot per method — used in 1.1, 1.2, 1.3, 1.4.
- `@Transactional(propagation = REQUIRES_NEW)` for "fan-out is best-effort" — used in 1.6 (after spec deviation).
- `@EnabledIfSystemProperty(name="yeosal.boot-smoke")` for Testcontainers ITs — used in 1.1, 1.2, 1.3, 1.4.
- `<SubModeProvider subMode="...">` page wrapper, never per-leaf — used in 1.6, 1.7.
- AsyncStorage typed-boundary helper (`chatRead.ts` → `ritualStorage.ts`) — used in 1.7.

**These patterns are not in `project-context.md` as a coherent canon.** Each story dev rediscovers them from prior stories' Completion Notes. Codifying them would prevent C6-class drift.

### I4 — Foundation stories should ship the *enforcement*, not just the artifact

Story 1.5 didn't just ship `tokens.json` — it shipped the schema validator, the Checkstyle hex-literal guard, the brand-voice-lint Rule 1 hard gate, and the codegen pipeline. Result: Stories 1.6 and 1.7 cannot drift even if they try. Contrast with `project-context.md` rules (constructor injection, single `@RestControllerAdvice`, etc.) — these are documented but not enforced, and drift happens (1.6 spec deviation; review findings on every story).

**Lesson:** if a rule matters, encode it as a lint / Checkstyle / ArchUnit gate. Documentation alone is not load-bearing.

### I5 — Privacy filters belong on the server, exclusively

Story 1.3 AC3 enforces a 4-conjunct mask predicate server-side. Architecture §4.14 quote landed verbatim in the story: *"Never trust the FE to filter privacy."* Story 1.2's two-channel realtime fan-out (immediate private + delayed broad via `pending_realtime_broadcasts`) holds the same line.

For Epic 2 (Spectator Mode), the same discipline applies to chat-write rejection (NFR-9.2.5) and record visibility (FR-8.2.4). Story 2.1's AC explicitly calls out "double-enforcement of NFR-9.2.5 to an explicit owned AC" — promoted from implicit to explicit per readiness review.
